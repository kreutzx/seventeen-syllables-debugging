## The haiku (5-7-5, 63 characters)

> three a.m. cursor
> the log says nothing at all
> missing semicolon

Syllables: three(1) a.m.(2) cursor(2) = 5; the(1) log(1) says(1) nothing(2) at(1) all(1) = 7; missing(2) semicolon(3) = 5.

## Why this haiku

One concrete picture at the centre — a cursor blinking at 3 a.m., a log that says nothing — and the last line turns the poem: the whole lost night was one missing semicolon. Every word earns its place.

Rejected candidates:

- "breakpoint blinking red / I change one small line and pray / now two bugs remain" (5-7-5) — a true debugging law, but the turn ("two bugs") is a familiar punchline; the semicolon reveal is sharper and more specific.
- "works on my machine / but production disagrees / Friday deploy dies" (5-7-5) — iconic opening line, but "works on my machine" is a stock phrase; the cursor-at-3am picture is more vivid and less borrowed.
